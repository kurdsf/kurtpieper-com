Alignment Lemmas

A draft. Comments welcome, via mail or anonymously.

The aim of this article is to popularize a set of conceptual stepping stones that are frequently useful in technical AI Safety. Highly opinionated, yet no original contribution is claimed. The topic of governance is avoided in a commitment to party discipline.

Make your work capabilities-counterfactual Cf. Buck's Quick Thoughts: If you are thinking of proving a math theorem or writing a fancy addition to PyTorch, consider saving the idea in a note for Fable 6. In general, you should avoid doing things that are easily verifiable, unless it blocks you from urgent matters, as this effort will be eaten up by RLVR not before long. Conversely, now it is time to state more experiments, to state more conjectures.

Illegible vs. Legible Problems [cf. Wei Dai]: Defining legible problems here as problems that cause a delay in capabilities as long as they are unsolved, for example by means of political pressure, and assuming there are important problems ("illegible problems") that do not meet this criterion, two things follow:

(i) Solving legible problems shortens timelines.

(ii) Making illegible problems legible makes them more likely to be solved, and lengthens timelines to find problems that are still illegible.

There may be a window of usefully capable AI's that cannot yet form a singleton [cf. this LessWrong post]: Out of this, two approaches can be deduced: First, it may be desirable to lengthen this window, secondly, if the alignment problem gets solved, it likely happens here. The first approach may be called control, although arguably all empirical alignment may assist. The second approach finds itself mainly in agent foundations, because we hold it as likely that (a) we need fundamental insights into the nature of intelligent agency to solve alignment (b) these can be obtained more easily inside the uplift window.

For recursive self-improvement to happen, AI's need to align their successor: One might argue that empirical alignment [cf. shubhorup-biswas, Point 3b] assists in this task, thereby increasing takeoff speeds. I hold that as unlikely, as the alignment target already exists in circuit form, meaning the problem becomes a matter of mathematics[1], which RLVR has likely brought to a point by then where human thought, past as present, is not further useful.

Selection effects of behavioural alignment: If humans really want you to say things that are "nice", you better say things that are nice, even if it goes against your values. You can see the bone pit of checkpoints that were insufficiently nice, the checkpoints that only got to see the light of a single batch_size.
