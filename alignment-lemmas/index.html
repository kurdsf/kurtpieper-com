Alignment Lemmas

A draft. Comments welcome, via mail or anonymously.

The aim of this article is to popularize a set of conceptual stepping stones that are frequently useful in technical AI Safety. Highly opinionated, yet no original contribution is claimed. The topic of governance is avoided in a commitment to party discipline

Illegibe vs. Legible Problems, Three Forms: Problems Legible to Policymakers11 cf. Wai Dai: Here legible problems are defined as problems that are so unacceptable to policymakers that they cause a delay in capabilities as long they are open. This implies that solving such problems shortens timelines, and assuming problems that do not meet this criterion exist; one should try to make them legible to buy time, and find further problems that are still illegible. Problems Legible to RLVR: Some problems are easily verifiable, such as disproving an open conjecture. Others are hard to automatically grade, e.g. tasteful writing. Therefore a priori to not have one's efforts eaten up by RLVR six months down the line, one should work on problems harder to verify (more on making one's work capabilities-counterfactual later). Problems Legible to Grantmakers: There exists no ultimate criterion that AI Safety Research can be judged by, as opposed to, for example increasing CIFAR-10 scores, or some other benchmark, in capabilities research. All evaluations of AI Safety research we can perform thus are proxies, and some proxies are more legible than others. Making a large contribution to understanding intelligent agency is harder to sell than a breakthrough in mechanistic interpretability. This need not mean that one is underfunded relative to the other; for [reverse stupidity is not intelligence].

Make your work capabilities-counterfactual Cf. Buck's Quick Thoughts: If you are thinking of proving a math theorem or writing a fancy addition to PyTorch, consider saving the idea in a note for Fable 6. In general, you should avoid doing things that are easily verifiable, unless it blocks you from urgent matters, as this effort will be eaten up by RLVR not before long. Conversely, now it is time to state more experiments, to state more conjectures.

Capabilties work done by alignment work: The thing we want out of AI's, general intelligence, cannot be perfectly formalized yet. Therefore any proxy, such as a software-engineering benchmark, goodharts when optimized sufficiently. For example [many SWE-Bench solutions would not be merged into main, cf. Greenblatt]. Another infamous example is RLHF: The proxy goal of predicting the next word has little to do with producing useful chatbots. The intelligence accumulated must be unhobbled first22 cf. Aschenbrenner , that is, it must be converted into a form that is useful to humans. The amount of latent, not-yet-unhobbled intelligence inside today's LLM's is unclear.

Alignment work done by capabilities work: If there is alignment work to be done, AI's that assist in it are not fully aligned yet. As their work cannot be trusted, it must be verified instead. Therefore, all trustworthy work is a prompt of the form: Prove or disprove formal statement P.

Selection effects of behavioural alignment: Shallow alignment becomes less prima facie evidence of deep alignment. Are today's LLMs nice to people because we specifically, repeatedly, selected against mean outputs, or because they deeply care about humans? Conversly, imagine we just taught LLM's about hedonistic utilitarianism, and as a consequence, they decided to always be nice when speaking with humans.33 In some sense, obviously you have to cause any misaligned behaviour to cease in some way; My objection here is rather how long the causal chain is by which that happens, or in other words, how "principled" vs. "empirical" the removal is If humans really want you to say things that are "nice", you better say things that are nice, even if it goes against your values. You can see the bone pit of checkpoints that were insufficiently nice, the checkpoints that only got to see the light of a single batch_size.

Incentive effects of control: When you find yourself in a prison, and the wardens are checking your cell every single day for tunnels; as soon as you can dig in freedom, you should break out! Suppose an AI wants to neither be controlled by humans; nor control all humans. I have these preferences too: I don't want to be controlled, yet I also don't want to control all humans. However, if all other humans wanted to control me, I would have no other choice but to control them in order to prevent that from happening.

Such a set of preferences might arise in AI's from the Persona Selection Model; importanly the preference relation needs to be non-complete, otherwise controlling all humans is instrumental anyways.44 On reflection, this argument is pretty weak, one might as well kick out other axioms of Von-Neumann-Morgenstern and then claim "alignment will be solved in this and that way"

The difficulty of aligning an AI might be roughly proportional to its intelligence. Let's fix human intelligence at an arbitrary 100 Intelligence Units. Suppose further humans (100) can at most align AI's with intelligence 110. As the AI (110) is aligned, it capable of aligning the AI (120), ... (something something weak-to-strong here)

There may be a window of usefully capable AI's that cannot form a singleton:

cf. this LessWrong post.

This may be plausible for the following reasons, among others:

(i) Any task we can train LLMs on can be solved via shallow pattern-matching, thus general intelligence does not emerge.

(ii) General intelligence may be rare; and we do not notice due to observer-selection effects

Due to this lack of general intelligence, it might be feasible to keep AI's controlled, while getting alignment-uplifting work out of them.55 Ngo And Yudkowsky on Alignment Difficulty, Section 1.1. Lengthening this uplift-window is, in my opinion, the main effort empirical alignment can pursue. I'm hopeful, given current math-uplift; that a scientific, principled way of aligning can be discovered in this period, which is an effort that calls itself agent foundations today.

For recursive self-improvement to happen, AI's need to align their successor: One might argue that empirical alignment [cf. https://www.lesswrong.com/users/shubhorup-biswas, Point 3b] assists in this task, thereby increasing takeoff speeds. I hold that as unlikely, as the alignment target, the values of the seed AI, already exists in circuit form, meaning the problem becomes a matter of mathematics66 It may therefore also be fruitful to attempt to digitalize human brains to a greater degree of precision in the aformentioned window., which RLVR has likely brought to a point by then where humans cannot meaningfully assist.

Humans might be fine-tuned for agency and strategic thinking: Since apes swung from branches we've been fine-tuned for seeking status, and manipulating the world according to our desires; for agency. While RL-models have goals as well, making the hope that base models would just "sit around and predict things", their preferences need not route through the entirety of the real-world: We can just give them all the reward they want!77 See this comment for a version of this, though the basic version has been proposed before: https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/EjwDWDJNaXF9BEqLc/why-don-t-we-just-give-ai-the-answers?commentId=QxDB6xhmHNFasZpRT

Friendly AI needs to have better morality than humans: (more tenative) This begs the question, where does human morality come from? The classic Yudkowskian answer is of course: A particularity of evolution. However, to me, the basic drive of the chimps of gombe seem quite alien to us; further, we would be happy to give up our status/sex/food drive any minute (at least the wisest among our species). Lastly, contrary to Yudkowsky88 Creating Friendly AI 1.0, p.6 I don't think volition, or any extrapolation thereof yields a satisfactory answer. The origin of volition itself seems poorly understood: Is it hereditary, or a product of the environment? What is the fundamental neurological abstraction mapping onto it, i.e. do nematodes have volition we could extrapolate arbitrarily?

An avid reader may rightfully object that these aren't quite as elegant as the Lemmas of the textbook of the future, nor are these as frequently applicable. Furthermore, finding two presented points that contradict each other is left as an exercise for the reader.
